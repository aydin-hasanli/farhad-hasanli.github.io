About

Research biography

I am a doctoral researcher in Aerospace Engineering at Texas A&M University. My research interests center on computational fluid dynamics and the development of accurate, stable, and efficient numerical methods for challenging flow problems.

My work has focused on high-order discontinuous Galerkin spectral element methods (DGSEM), including entropy-stable split-form discretizations, viscous formulations, shock sensing, and subcell finite-volume blending. I am particularly interested in methods that retain high-order accuracy in smooth regions while remaining robust near shocks and other under-resolved flow features.

More broadly, I am interested in numerical methods for partial differential equations, compressible and incompressible flow, turbulence, direct numerical simulation, scientific software, and high-performance computing.

Research

Areas of interest

High-Order CFD

High-order spatial discretizations for compressible and incompressible flow, with emphasis on accuracy, robustness, and efficient implementation.

DG / DGSEM

Discontinuous Galerkin spectral element methods based on nodal collocation, Legendre–Gauss–Lobatto points, and high-order polynomial representations.

Entropy Stability

Split-form and entropy-stable discretizations designed to improve nonlinear stability for under-resolved and compressible flow simulations.

Shock Capturing

Sensors, artificial stabilization, and subcell finite-volume blending for robust treatment of shocks and discontinuities.

Turbulence & DNS

Numerical simulation of turbulent flows, including high-resolution and direct numerical simulation approaches.

Scientific Computing

Parallel numerical software, performance-oriented implementation, and scalable computational methods for fluid dynamics.

Research Work

Selected work

Numerical solver

High-Order DGSEM CFD Solver

Development of a high-order discontinuous Galerkin spectral element solver for the Euler and Navier–Stokes equations in one, two, and three dimensions.

The solver includes entropy-stable split forms, viscous discretization, multiple explicit Runge–Kutta schemes, shock sensing, and subcell finite-volume blending.

Stabilization

Subcell Shock Capturing

Investigation of robust shock-capturing strategies that blend high-order DGSEM with lower-order subcell finite-volume methods in troubled regions.

Flow simulation

Canonical CFD Test Problems

Numerical studies involving shock tubes, double Mach reflection, Kelvin–Helmholtz instability, forward-facing step, Sedov blast, triple-point problems, and lid-driven cavity flow.

Current interests

Turbulent Channel Flow

Interest in high-resolution wall-bounded turbulence simulations and direct numerical simulation using high-order methods and modern high-performance computing architectures.
